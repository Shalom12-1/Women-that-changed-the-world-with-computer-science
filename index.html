Mary Kenneth Keller, B.V.M. was an American Catholic religious sister, educator and pioneer in computer science.
She was one of the first people, and the first woman to earn a Ph.D. in computer science in the United States. Keller and Irving C. Tang were the first two recipients of computer science doctorates.

She discovered Nerve Growth Factor (NGF)—a protein crucial for the growth, maintenance, and survival of nerve cells.

She invented the term because people used to view writing code as a lesser, informal skill compared to hardware engineering.

She popularized the term "debugging" after her team pulled a real moth out of a relay in the Harvard Mark II computer in 1947 and taped it into the logbook.
